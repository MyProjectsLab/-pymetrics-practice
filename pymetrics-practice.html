What is a Pymetrics assessment?

Pymetrics uses short tasks to explore how you pay attention and make decisions. The tasks can involve memory, quick responses, planning or choices about risk and reward.

Pymetrics and Harver: what is the connection?

Harver offers pymetrics game-based assessments and acquired pymetrics in 2022. Harver also offers other kinds of assessments, so a Harver invitation does not tell you on its own which tasks you will take. Read the assessment name and instructions in your invitation.

How each practice test works

Harver describes broad areas such as attention and decision-making in its Pymetrics overview. These notes explain our practice tests. “Focus” means what a task involves, not an official Harver trait score. One task cannot establish your personality.

Open a practice test for its full rules and example.

  • Stop

    Play: Respond only when the target cue appears.

    Focus: Attention and holding back a response.

    Watch for: Missing the target or responding to the wrong cue.

  • Balloons

    Play: Pump to build simulated winnings, or collect them.

    Focus: Deciding how much risk to take.

    Watch for: A burst loses that balloon’s uncollected winnings.

  • Keypresses

    Play: Repeat a response between the start and stop signals.

    Focus: Effort, rhythm and following signals.

    Watch for: Starting early or continuing after the stop signal.

  • Arrows

    Play: Choose the arrow direction that matches the current rule.

    Focus: Staying focused when there are distractions.

    Watch for: The rule may change which arrows you should follow.

  • Lengths

    Play: Decide whether the mouth shown is short or long.

    Focus: Spotting small differences and responding to feedback.

    Watch for: The two pictures can look very similar.

  • Digits

    Play: Watch the digits, then enter them in the same order.

    Focus: Remembering a short sequence.

    Watch for: Knowing the digits is not enough: their order matters.

  • Cards

    Play: Choose a deck and see each gain or loss.

    Focus: Learning from outcomes and choosing what to try next.

    Watch for: A large gain can be followed by a larger loss.

  • Easy or Hard

    Play: Choose an easier or harder task for a possible reward.

    Focus: Weighing effort against a possible payoff.

    Watch for: The harder task does not guarantee a reward.

  • Towers

    Play: Move the disks to match the target arrangement.

    Focus: Planning a few moves ahead.

    Watch for: A rushed move may need to be undone.

  • Money Exchange 1

    Play: Choose how much simulated money to send, then see the return.

    Focus: Choosing when the return is uncertain.

    Watch for: You may get back more or less than you sent.

  • Money Exchange 2

    Play: Choose what to give or take, or watch a partner’s choice. Then rate fairness.

    Focus: Sharing and judging how fair an exchange feels.

    Watch for: Read the round’s instructions before choosing an amount.

  • Faces

    Play: Choose an emotion using the portrait and any situation shown.

    Focus: Reading visual cues and considering context.

    Watch for: A face alone cannot prove what someone feels.

Research behind the tasks

Researchers use simple tasks to study attention, memory, planning and choices. Harver says its Pymetrics tasks draw on this research.

Read the research sources
  • Harver’s assessment overview explains the provider’s approach.
  • Harver’s research white paper (PDF) describes its research background. This is provider material.
  • Lejuez and colleagues: Balloon Analogue Risk Task studies choices about risk.
  • Verbruggen and Logan: response inhibition studies responding and holding back in Go/No-Go and stop-signal tasks. These are different task types; our Stop practice uses Go/No-Go rules.

These sources explain related research tasks. They do not validate our practice tests or predict your hiring result.

What does practice tell me?

Your report describes this practice: for example accuracy, timing, moves or choices, depending on the task. It does not provide an official Pymetrics score, a personality profile, an employer benchmark or a hiring prediction. There is no universal pass score here and no promise of a better recruitment outcome.

How should I prepare?

  1. Check the task name, deadline, allowed device and accessibility instructions in your real invitation.
  2. Read the guide and try its example. Use a laptop or desktop for standard timed practice; each test explains its controls.
  3. Find a quiet place. Keep the practice tab in front and avoid switching apps during timed tasks.
  4. Look at your practice summary. Detailed practice records stay in this browser when storage is available; clearing site data removes them.
