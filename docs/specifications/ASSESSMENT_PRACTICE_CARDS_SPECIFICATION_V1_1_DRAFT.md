# Assessment Practice — Cards V1.1 working specification

**Status:** Agent-authored draft. Independent gameplay and numerical review are required before freeze or product code. This supersedes the rejected V1.0 working draft; neither draft is a shipped product or a claim of current Pymetrics equivalence.

**Proposed identity:** `cards-rules/1.1.0`, `cards-constructor/1.1.0`, `cards-forms/1.1.0`, `cards-events/1.1.0`, `cards-score/1.1.0`, `cards-report/1.1.0`, `cards-storage/1.1.0`, `cards-mock-adapter/1.1.0`.

## 1. Authority and reference boundary

The Master Strategy controls product scope. The [observable reference contract](../qa/cards-v1/CARDS-OBSERVABLE-CONTRACT-DRAFT.md) records the saved JobTestPrep video/screenshot evidence and the public-source limits. Match its observable setup and flow: **four persistent decks, 80 draws, simulated starting balance 2,000, one deck choice per draw, immediate signed outcome/balance, free switching, no mandatory Next, and a four-deck report**. Use original implementation, artwork, wording, outcome sequences and descriptive scoring. No provider probability, employer trait, pass score, calibration, or exact current Pymetrics equivalence is claimed. Cards has no Advanced Training in Public Beta 0.1.

## 2. Candidate journey

Guide: “Choose a deck to draw one card. Each card changes your practice balance by a displayed amount. Try to finish with as much practice money as you can. You can switch decks after any draw. The game ends after 80 draws.” State that the starting 2,000 is simulated, not a real loan or payment. Four persistent buttons have visible and accessible labels `Deck A` through `Deck D`; neutral symbols supplement letters, and color never carries identity. Show `Draw k of 80`, current balance, and one concise outcome line. The starting screen shows 2,000 and no outcome.

A separate non-scored four-draw coached example demonstrates a gain, loss, zero outcome, and switch; label its outcomes as examples. The comprehension check requires correct answers to: can a draw lose money (yes), can you switch decks (yes), is there a choice deadline (no), and how many draws (80); explain an error and allow retry. Neither example nor check consumes form exposure. Preflight confirms storage, local-save limits, interruption behavior and accessible controls. Then show a brief ready screen with a deliberate `Next` action to begin the scored run; this action is the scored Start and reserves the form. The reference recording shows a ready/Next screen before the 2,000-balance board; no Next is required between draws.

The candidate clicks/taps/keyboard-activates any deck once per draw. The selected card is revealed with signed gain/loss or zero and an updated balance. The same four deck buttons become available for the next draw after the outcome is durably confirmed and visibly presented. **There is no Next button or automatic countdown.** The candidate can pause on the feedback for as long as desired. A low-motion text-first reveal is valid; no animation gate changes outcomes. If the saved reference's exact feedback dwell cannot be established, do not add a deadline or extra action. An optional Exit preserves a labeled incomplete prefix. The 80th draw opens a complete report only after its feedback is confirmed, the guarded `run_completed`/snapshot transaction commits, and authoritative readback verifies that result.

## 3. Original immutable forms and draw semantics

Six forms `CD1`–`CD6` are defined in `ASSESSMENT_PRACTICE_CARDS_FORMS_V1_1.json`. A form has an ID, seed, four-letter visible position order, four **independent 80-card sequences**, and a SHA-256 of its body. On draw `k`, selecting deck `d` reveals `decks[d][number of prior confirmed selections of d]`. Global turn number does not index an unchosen deck. Exactly one selected outcome is experienced per draw. No unchosen/future value is shown or treated as an event. All 80 draws may be from the same deck, so every deck sequence has 80 values. Decks remain selectable and keep their visible position throughout one run; a new form may use a different position order.

The checked-in generator `docs/qa/cards-v1/generate_v11.py` is authoritative for the original schedules. For form `CDi`, seed is unsigned 32-bit `0xCA4D1100+i`. Process decks A, B, C, D in that order. For each deck, concatenate four independently shuffled 20-card segments with one continuous xorshift32 stream; Fisher–Yates descends from 19 to 1 using `next_u32 % (index+1)`. Transition: `x ^= (x << 13) & 0xffffffff; x ^= x >> 17; x ^= (x << 5) & 0xffffffff`, masking to 32 bits. The deck segment multisets are:

| Deck | 20-card multiset | 20-card net | 80-card net |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | fourteen `+100`, six `−100` | +800 | +3,200 |
| B | eighteen `+100`, two `−1,100` | −400 | −1,600 |
| C | sixteen `+25`, two `0`, two `−25` | +350 | +1,400 |
| D | nineteen `+50`, one `−200` | +750 | +3,000 |

These values and pattern strengths are **original practice design values**, not JobTestPrep or Pymetrics distributions. Their broad feedback scale and deck risk pattern reflect the saved practice recording without copying its hidden sequence or claiming equivalent probabilities. Do not teach one deck as universally best; realized mixed paths depend on observed outcomes and deck choices. The six visible orders, in form order, are `ABCD`, `BCAD`, `CADB`, `DBAC`, `ADCB`, `BDCA`. Runtime loads and verifies the checked-in bytes; it never generates or reshuffles forms. JSON is UTF-8, two-space indented, stable key order and final LF. Each form's body digest excludes its own `formSha256` field.

Installation exposure assigns CD1 through CD6 in order, then cycles and labels repeats. A scored Start atomically reserves a form and increments `nextExposureOrdinal`; example/check/preflight do not. A timed-out Start retries the same immutable request ID and exact payload after authoritative readback and never consumes a second ordinal. A prior-document incomplete run cannot be transferred or resumed and does not block a new Start with the next exposure. Deleting all creates a new installation and resets exposure after a clear warning.

## 4. Input, feedback and clocks

One trusted native `click` of an enabled deck button is the canonical draw command. `keydown`, `keyup`, `pointerdown` and touch traces classify/suppress, never append separate draws. Matching fresh Enter/Space becomes `keyboard`; matching pointer activation becomes `pointer` or `touch`; a trusted activation without a matching trace is admitted as `assistive_or_unknown`. Reject script-dispatched clicks and modified, composing or repeated keyboard activations. Focus/target change, canceled pointer and stale trace cannot classify later activation. Latch immutable `{runId,turn,deck,deckDraw,commandId,source}` synchronously, disable all decks, and serialize the durable write; double click or late callback cannot create a second draw.

After the choice is committed, verify the exact committed command and manifest-derived outcome/balance before displaying feedback. Display one signed outcome and updated balance through visible text and one polite live announcement; move focus predictably to that feedback. Append and confirm `feedback_shown` after actual display. Only then re-enable the deck buttons for the next draw and focus the first deck in the current visible order. A pending or failed choice/feedback write says `Save unconfirmed`, keeps decks disabled and offers exact-command retry/recovery **only while the same document still owns an active run with admission open**. Do not infer failure from a callback timeout. For the 80th draw, atomically verify 80 matching choices and 80 confirmed feedback events and write `run_completed` with its immutable snapshot in one guarded transaction; open the report only after authoritative readback.

**Committed but unconfirmed-feedback draw:** A durable `choice_committed` is a saved draw even if interruption happens before `feedback_shown`. The incomplete review counts that draw and its manifest-derived balance, but labels it `Outcome saved; feedback display was not confirmed during play`. Absence of `feedback_shown` does not prove the candidate never saw it. Review may reveal the saved outcome only after authoritative readback, never by reviving the interrupted game. Keep separate `committedDrawCount` and `feedbackSeenCount` (the latter is the contiguous count of matching confirmed `feedback_shown` events, not proof that the candidate perceived the announcement); do not treat unconfirmed feedback as evidence for a later in-game decision. An observed interruption, Exit or ownership loss closes local admission immediately; a committed guarded run-state transition terminalizes the run. After admission closes, abort still-abortable pending `draw_ready`, choice, feedback and terminal writes; an irreversible write may commit but its callback cannot re-enable decks, advance or submit completion, and only authoritative readback may count its bytes. If the choice write itself is uncertain, show only the previously verified counts and `Save unconfirmed` until exact authoritative readback settles whether that draw committed. Deletion/replacement withdraws the review and prevents late disclosure or write.

No choice or whole-game deadline exists. A single document's monotonic clock records draw readiness, choice, feedback and optional descriptive latency; negative/non-finite/decreasing values fail closed. New document origins are never subtracted. Browser-visible readiness requires a confirmed rendered deck surface before first or subsequent draw admission. A missing render callback keeps controls disabled and offers bounded recovery; it cannot silently start a draw.

## 5. Recovery, storage and validity

Use one versioned IndexedDB installation root with append-only run journals, form exposures, immutable completed snapshots, history and tombstones. Each run has immutable run and originating-document IDs, installation ID/generation `G`, owner epoch `E`, monotone play revision `R` and one authoritative state: `active`, `interrupted`, `exited`, `invalid`, `complete` or tombstoned. Installation-wide exact request-ID/payload records include Start. Inside a transaction, first reject a stale generation or tombstone; then resolve exact-request idempotency **before** applying expected-revision checks to a genuinely new request. A committed retry of the same ID and payload returns its recorded result without writing bytes again; the same ID with a different payload fails; deletion never discloses a prior request result. New play/terminal writes require current `G`, originating document, `E`, `R`, `active` state, no tombstone and an open local admission latch. Interruption/Exit/ownership-revocation writes remain permitted after the local latch closes and check the same durable authority, with their specified state transition. The successful guarded transition is the ordering point; callback delivery, wall timestamps and screen paint are not. Use exclusive per-run ownership while accepting draws; another tab may read history but cannot advance the run. Ownership loss/transfer in an active run atomically revokes `E`, sets `invalid` and appends one matching event under the same root transaction, leaving no active interval for a new owner; if completion committed first, do not reclassify it. No in-game ownership transfer or resume is allowed. Individual deletion atomically tombstones the run; Delete all atomically replaces the installation and removes prior journals/exposures. A failed or rejected transaction changes no root bytes. Whichever guarded write commits first governs a race, but deletion after completion still withdraws display authority. A late callback or readback cannot resurrect a deleted/replaced run or publish stale saved status. The UI claims success only after authoritative readback.

When `visibilitychange(hidden)`, `pagehide`, freeze, ownership loss or Exit is **observed** in the originating document, synchronously latch admission closed, disable controls, withdraw any pending complete claim and abort every still-abortable pending readiness, choice, feedback or terminal transaction before awaiting storage. Do not submit or retry play or terminal writes after this latch. Attempt the corresponding idempotent interruption/exit state transition; ownership loss uses the single atomic epoch-revocation/invalid transition above, not a later invalidation by the old epoch. A signal never delivered to JavaScript is not observed evidence. If a non-complete transition commits first, a later play/terminal write fails its state/revision/owner guard and changes no bytes. An already unabortable play/terminal transaction may still commit despite subsequent observation; its exact journal/terminal bytes count only after readback. If terminal commits first, later interruption/ownership loss does not reclassify the completed result. Until readback settles an irreversible write, its outcome is uncertain. Resolve the race by guarded durable transition order, never callback or wall-clock order.

If interruption/exit persistence fails or its callback is undelivered after admission closes, keep that document closed and retry only the same interruption/exit request while it exists; never authorize a terminal retry. After reload, a prior-document run without an authoritative terminal snapshot is **read-only incomplete**, even if its durable state remains `active` with 80 choices and 80 feedback events: its originating-document ID and owner epoch cannot be claimed by a new document. Do not synthesize `run_completed` across document epochs. Preserve the last authoritative contiguous committed-choice prefix and separate feedback-seen prefix. If no interruption marker was saved, label the cause `Interruption/save status unconfirmed`, not proven interrupted. If the root is unavailable, assert no count or balance. A new run reserves the next form despite an orphaned prior-document `active` record. Before a local interruption latch, ambiguous terminal delivery may retry only the same immutable terminal request in the same active owning document after authoritative readback; after the latch, readback only. Until exact readback proves terminal completion, show `Completion save unconfirmed` and no complete report. Mock interruption restarts Cards from a fresh exposure, consistent with between-game-only mock resume.

A complete valid result requires supported form digest, exactly 80 contiguous one-per-turn choices, 80 matching confirmed `feedback_shown` events, matching deck-local indices and manifest outcomes, coherent balance chain, valid readiness/timestamps, no earlier committed interruption/exit/invalid transition, and an authoritative `complete` transition with `run_completed` event and immutable terminal snapshot in the same transaction. Missing or contradictory evidence fails closed. Incomplete reviews use only the last authoritative contiguous committed-choice prefix and never report a complete score; they identify any final committed choice lacking play-time feedback and distinguish an unconfirmed interruption cause.

Required versioned events: `run_started` (form/exposure/starting balance), `draw_ready` (turn/render stamp), `choice_committed` (turn/deck/deckDraw/signed outcome/prior and new balance/command/source/latency), `feedback_shown` (command/display stamp), `lifecycle_changed`, `run_interrupted`/`run_exited`/`run_invalid`, `run_completed` (80 draws/final balance), `report_viewed`, `history_viewed`. All carry installation/run identity, rule/form versions and digest, ordered sequence, run revision and monotonic/wall context as applicable. Only selected committed choices affect money.

## 6. Report, history, mock and accessibility

For confirmed choices `v₁…v₈₀`, `gains=Σmax(vᵢ,0)`, `losses=Σmax(−vᵢ,0)`, and `ending balance=2,000+gains−losses`. A zero outcome belongs to neither gain nor loss but counts as a draw. Per-deck chosen counts sum to 80; per-deck gains/losses sum to global gains/losses. `switches=Σ[i=2..80]1(deckᵢ≠deckᵢ₋₁)` with denominator 79 adjacent pairs. Trace shows global turn, chosen deck, that deck's draw index, signed outcome and resulting balance. Never show unchosen sequences, counterfactual earnings, percentile, employer score, risk trait or optimality claim. Display the form ID and repeated-form flag. Incomplete review labels every observed figure `through draw k of 80` and does not normalize to 80.

History lists date, form, status, committed draw count, feedback-seen count when they differ, and authoritative complete or prefix balance. If the authoritative root is unavailable, show no asserted count or balance; if no interruption marker was saved, distinguish the unconfirmed cause from a proven interruption. A complete report can reopen after reload. Report/history views append idempotent local events after actual display in a **separate metadata stream/revision**, without changing the immutable play journal or terminal snapshot. Those metadata writes require the current installation generation, run identity and no tombstone, but not active-play ownership; another tab may read history. Replay starts a new run. Before publishing report/history data, verify a readback token containing generation, run ID, tombstone status, play revision and terminal-snapshot identity; recheck it immediately before rendering and invalidate it on known deletion, replacement, navigation, focus loss or relevant notification. Recheck on entry/focus/notification and after delayed readback. A cross-tab deletion cannot be guaranteed to erase already painted data before its notification arrives; once observed, withdraw it, and never republish deleted bytes through a later action or callback. Mock identifies Cards as Game 8, passes only authoritative `complete_valid` status to the orchestrator, and never resumes an interrupted in-game prefix. No optional analytics or server-side candidate storage is part of this packet.

Use native deck buttons, visible text and letter/symbol identity, 44×44 CSS-pixel targets, focus visibility, one outcome announcement, reduced motion, high contrast, 200% zoom and 320 CSS-pixel usability. A screen reader must discover the turn, current balance, four decks and signed feedback. No color or animation may be needed to understand an outcome. Artwork, text, icons and sounds are original.
