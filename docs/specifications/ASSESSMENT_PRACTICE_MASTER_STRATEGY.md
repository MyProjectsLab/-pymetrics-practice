# Assessment Practice — Master Strategy

## Document authority

This is the authoritative product and execution strategy for Assessment Practice.
It consolidates the Corrected Master MVP Strategy and the Lean Execution
Amendment. Where the earlier documents conflicted, the Lean Execution Amendment
controls.

This document defines product scope, positioning, release standards, architecture,
execution sequence, evidence boundaries, and implementation governance. It is not
an exact specification for any individual game. Each game requires a separate,
founder-approved specification before implementation.

## Status language

- **Verified fact:** Supported by the supplied reference inventory or employer
  research.
- **Binding product decision:** Approved scope that implementation must follow.
- **Hypothesis:** Something the beta must test.
- **Unresolved risk:** A release dependency or decision requiring resolution.

---

## 1. Executive product decision

Assessment Practice will launch as a free, local-first candidate-preparation web
application with three integrated layers.

### Layer 1 — Complete Practice Library

All twelve working game families must be playable at public launch:

1. Balloons
2. Cards
3. Easy or Hard
4. Stop
5. Towers
6. Keypresses
7. Digits
8. Arrows
9. Lengths
10. Money Exchange 1
11. Money Exchange 2
12. Faces

Every game requires:

- An original game guide.
- Rules and controls.
- A coached example.
- A comprehension check.
- A realistic practice simulation.
- Versioned event recording.
- Basic descriptive results.
- A practical review.
- Replay and local history.
- Accessibility behavior and limitations.
- Clear scientific and product limitations.
- Original wording, visuals, stimuli, scoring, assets, and code.

### Layer 2 — Full Mock Assessment

Users must be able to complete all twelve games in one documented
assessment-style sequence with:

- Pre-assessment instructions.
- Device, storage, and interruption checks.
- A versioned Assessment Practice game order.
- Progress indication.
- A break policy.
- Resume and invalid-session rules.
- An end-of-assessment summary.
- Individual game results.
- Independence and non-proprietary-scoring language.

The mock does not produce a composite personality, employability, employer-fit,
or pass/fail score.

### Layer 3 — Advanced Training Mode

Advanced Training Mode will launch for:

- Stop.
- Balloons.
- Towers.

For these games, the complete loop is:

`Baseline → task evidence → strength and trade-off → deterministic drill → focused exercise → unseen Assessment Practice form → baseline comparison → progress`

The other nine games launch with complete Practice Mode and basic descriptive
reporting. The product must not imply that personalized training exists where it
has not been designed.

The interface will state:

- **Practice available** for all twelve games.
- **Advanced training available** for Stop, Balloons, and Towers.
- **Advanced training planned** for additional games, without a promised date.

## 2. Audience, problem, and positioning

### Initial audience

The initial audience is:

> English-speaking undergraduate and recent-graduate candidates preparing for
> early-career finance recruitment, with investment banking as the first
> specialized pathway.

The product is not restricted to the United States. Employer and assessment
guidance must be qualified by programme, role, geography, recruiting year, and
evidence-review date. The candidate's actual invitation is always authoritative.

### Candidate problem

The initial user faces three connected problems:

1. **Format uncertainty:** The user does not know how the games will feel under
   time pressure.
2. **Opaque feedback:** A number or trait label does not explain which actions
   produced a result.
3. **No focused improvement path:** Generic tips do not identify what to practise
   next or whether behavior changed on a new form.

### Final positioning

> Practise the complete assessment-style game set, understand how you performed,
> and receive deeper personalized training for supported games—without claims
> about employer pass profiles or hiring decisions.

Supporting message:

> All twelve games are available for realistic practice. Stop, Balloons, and
> Towers also include action-level analysis, targeted drills, unseen retests, and
> progress tracking.

### Product boundary

The product helps candidates:

- Learn task rules and controls.
- Become familiar with assessment-style pressure.
- Review performance on Assessment Practice tasks.
- Practise supported task strategies.
- Compare results across Assessment Practice forms.

It does not:

- Reproduce proprietary Pymetrics scoring.
- Reveal an employer's preferred profile.
- Diagnose personality.
- Teach candidates to fake behavioral preferences.
- Predict selection or workplace performance.
- Guarantee improvement.
- Claim endorsement by an employer, Harver, Pymetrics, or JobTestPrep.

### Library organization

The library may group games by task demand without presenting the groups as

#### Decisions and learning

- Balloons.
- Cards.
- Easy or Hard.

#### Attention, response, and perception

- Stop.
- Keypresses.
- Arrows.
- Lengths.

#### Memory and planning

- Digits.
- Towers.

#### Social decisions and interpretation

- Money Exchange 1.
- Money Exchange 2.
- Faces.

Each game card shows:

- Practice availability.
- Advanced-training availability.
- Approximate duration.
- Recommended input device.
- Accessibility notes.
- Completion status.
- Latest valid result.
- Replay action.

### Entry journey

1. **Landing:** Complete-library promise, Advanced Training distinction, time
   estimate, independence statement, and Start Free action.
2. **Goal:** Investment banking or general early-career finance preparation.
3. **Invitation check:** Provider, game, interview, and device information, with
   an invitation-first warning.
4. **Device, privacy, and accessibility check:** Browser, screen, input, timing,
   storage, interruption, and accessibility settings.
5. **Route choice:** Full mock, individual game, resume mock, resume training, or
   progress.

### Individual Practice Mode

1. Select a game.
2. Read the guide.
3. Learn the rules and controls.
4. Complete the coached example.
5. Pass the comprehension check.
6. Complete the simulation.
7. Receive the basic report.
8. Review practical observations and limitations.
9. Replay or return to the library.
10. For Stop, Balloons, or Towers, optionally enter Advanced Training Mode.

### Advanced Training Mode

1. Complete or select a valid baseline.
2. Review action-level task evidence.
3. See one prioritized task pattern.
4. Review the possible strength, trade-off, and limitations.
5. Receive the deterministic drill.
6. Complete the focused exercise.
7. Review drill execution.
8. Complete an unseen Assessment Practice form.
9. Compare baseline and retest.
10. Receive a next-step recommendation.
11. View local longitudinal progress.

## 6. Full twelve-game mock

### Evidence boundary

The supplied reference inventory documents individual game mechanics and the
cross-product path from library to report. It does not provide reliable evidence
of a real cross-game battery order.

Therefore, Assessment Practice will use an original sequence for usability and
state clearly:

> Real assessment order may vary by employer, programme, geography, and year.
> This is an original Assessment Practice mock sequence for preparation purposes.

### Public Beta 0.1 sequence

1. Keypresses
2. Stop
3. Digits
4. Arrows
5. Balloons
6. Towers
7. Lengths
8. Cards
9. Easy or Hard
10. Money Exchange 1
11. Money Exchange 2
12. Faces

The rationale is to begin with a short input-orientation task, vary task demands,
place a break at the midpoint, and keep the related Money Exchange games together.
The sequence is versioned. A later change creates a new mock version.

### Pre-assessment instructions

Show:

- Twelve-game count.
- Estimated duration.
- Input and device requirements.
- Break and interruption rules.
- Resume policy.
- Independence statement.
- Non-proprietary-scoring statement.

### Progress and break policy

- Show the current, completed, and remaining games.
- Show the scheduled break location.
- Do not show a live overall score.
- Offer one optional break after Game 6.
- Recommend no more than 10 minutes.
- Record the break duration.
- Permit additional accommodation breaks and record them.
- Do not pause within a game unless its approved specification permits it.

### Resume and invalid-session policy

- Save after every completed game.
- Resume only between games.
- Label a mock resumed within 24 hours as **resumed**.
- Label a mock resumed later as **multi-session practice**.
- Restart an interrupted game using a fresh form where available.
- Preserve invalid results for review, but do not use them as baselines.

Invalidating conditions may include:

- Material tab hiding or browser suspension.
- Timing failure.
- Mid-game input-device change.
- Mid-game accessibility-setting change.
- Missing required trials.
- Unsupported browser behavior.

### End summary

Show:

- Completed, invalid, and incomplete games.
- A basic result card for every game.
- Advanced-training availability for the supported games.
- A recommended next action.
- No composite employer, employability, or personality score.

## 7. Basic descriptive report standard

Every game report contains:

1. Completion and validity status.
2. Form and scoring version.
3. Device, input, interruption, and accessibility context.
4. Primary descriptive outcomes.
5. Condition or task-block breakdown.
6. Timing or consistency information where relevant.
7. Practical observations limited to the task.
8. Replay.
9. Limitations.
10. Advanced-training status.

### Game-specific results

| Game | Required basic descriptive results |
|---|---|
| Balloons | Collected and lost value, collected and burst counts, pumps by category, and block comparison |
| Cards | Total gains and losses, ending amount, choices by deck, switching, and outcome trace |
| Easy or Hard | Easy and hard choices, completion rates, offered probabilities and rewards, earnings, and timing |
| Stop | Hits, misses, false alarms, correct withholds, response time, and block results |
| Towers | Completion, total and optimal moves, excess moves, planning time, solution time, and undo/reset use |
| Keypresses | First valid press, press count and rate, threshold time, and premature or late presses |
| Digits | Maximum recalled length, accuracy by length, serial-position errors, substitutions, and transpositions |
| Arrows | Accuracy, errors, omissions, response time, and stimulus-condition breakdown |
| Lengths | Accuracy by category, response time, response tendency, and block performance |
| Money Exchange 1 | Transfer, return, final outcomes, fairness rating, and mathematical explanation |
| Money Exchange 2 | Give/take decision, final allocations, fairness rating, and action-rating comparison |
| Faces | Image-only and context accuracy, response time, confusion pairs, timeouts, and limitations |

Basic reports must not convert these measurements into stable-trait, morality,
intelligence, emotional-intelligence, employer-fit, or hiring conclusions.

## 8. Advanced Training Mode

### Common method

Each supported game uses deterministic, inspectable recommendation rules. Every
rule contains:

- Required measurements.
- Minimum valid data.
- Trigger and exclusion conditions.
- Recommended drill.
- Explanation language.
- Retest measurement.
- Version identifier.

Insufficient data produces no interpretation.

### Stop

Evidence may include:

- Hits, misses, and false alarms.
- Correct-response timing and variability.
- Task-block consistency.
- Post-error task behavior.

Example drill routing:

- High false alarms → response-withholding drill.
- Many misses with few false alarms → detection drill.
- Late-task decline → consistency drill.
- Accurate but variable timing → response-stability drill.

Retest with a new documented sequence.

### Balloons

Evidence may include:

- Pumps and collection points.
- Burst and collection outcomes.
- Behavior by independently designed condition.
- Post-burst adjustment.
- Differentiation and stability.

Example drill routing:

- Little condition differentiation → evidence-updating drill.
- Large short-lived burst reaction → outcome-reaction drill.
- Unstable collection points → decision-consistency drill.
- Decisions not changing with accumulated evidence → information-use drill.

Retest with unseen independently designed distributions. Until calibrated, state
that form difficulty may affect observed change.

### Towers

Evidence may include:

- Planning time.
- Total and excess moves.
- Reversals and repeated states.
- Undo behavior.
- Inter-move hesitation.
- Completion.

Example drill routing:

- Immediate start followed by reversals → plan-before-action drill.
- Long planning with efficient execution → timed-planning drill.
- Repeated state cycling → state-tracking drill.
- Breakdown on longer puzzles → multi-step sequencing drill.

Retest with unseen solvable puzzles and verified optimal paths.

### Comparison language

Allowed:

> Your false-alarm rate decreased from 18% to 9% on a new Assessment Practice
> sequence. Your median correct response time increased by 70 milliseconds.

Not allowed:

> Your impulse control improved by 50%.

The first describes task evidence and a trade-off. The second overstates the
inference.

## 9. Employer and role framework

### Investment banking

Investment banking is the first specialized pathway. It may contextualize reviewed
task observations using capabilities commonly relevant to analyst work, such as
accuracy, structured planning, checking, learning from feedback, and consistent
execution.

Role selection may change explanatory ordering and work examples. It must never
change task mechanics, scores, drill rules, or conclusions.

### Employer pages

Planned discovery content includes:

- JPMorgan.
- Blackstone for specifically confirmed programmes.
- An employer-evidence methodology page.

JPMorgan is a confirmed current banking example according to the supplied
research. Blackstone is an adjacent high-finance example, not an investment bank.
These pages are useful for discovery but must not delay the working game product.

### Employer-evidence classifications

1. **Current first-party confirmed**
2. **Current but limited or adjacent**
3. **Historical direct**
4. **Reported or programme-dependent**
5. **Unverified lead**

Every record requires:

- Normalized parent organization.
- Public display name.
- Evidence tier.
- Programme, role, geography, and year scope.
- Source and verification date.
- Approved wording.
- Limitations.

TheirStack and similar commercial databases are lead-discovery sources only. Their
rows are not employer counts, market-size evidence, or proof of current candidate
assessment use.

### Safe wording

For confirmed evidence:

> [Employer] states that candidates in [scope] may complete [named assessment].
> Processes can vary by role, programme, geography, and year. Check your
> invitation. Assessment Practice is independent and is not affiliated with or
> endorsed by [Employer], Harver, or Pymetrics.

For reported or programme-dependent evidence:

> Candidate reports and preparation sources have associated [Employer] with
> Pymetrics or similar game-based assessments, but we have not confirmed current
> use through a first-party employer source. Check your invitation before choosing
> preparation materials.

### Freshness

- Recheck current evidence every 90 days during active recruiting periods.
- Display the review date.
- Remove **current** language after 180 days without revalidation.
- Treat the candidate's invitation as authoritative.

## 12. Accessibility requirements

Accessibility settings must not silently imply that modified and standard task
forms are equivalent.

| Game | Public Beta 0.1 requirement |
|---|---|
| Balloons | Keyboard, pointer, and touch; color plus shape or texture; reduced motion; large controls |
| Cards | Persistent deck labels; keyboard access; screen-reader-readable outcomes; reduced motion |
| Easy or Hard | Remappable input; accessible countdown; motor-demand disclosure; separately labeled accommodated results |
| Stop | Shape plus color; remappable response; high contrast; reduced motion; high-distinction shape accommodation; extended-time mode postponed until after Public Beta 0.1 |
| Towers | No drag-only interaction; keyboard source/destination controls; patterned disks; accessible state description |
| Keypresses | Remappable input; dominant-hand option; motor limitation disclosure; separately labeled alternative-input results |
| Digits | High contrast; accessible entry; separately labeled visual and auditory forms; recorded timing accommodations |
| Arrows | Non-color rule cue; remappable controls; scalable stimuli; disclosed nonvisual-play limitations |
| Lengths | High contrast; appropriate magnification; non-color response cues; separately labeled visual accommodation form |
| Money Exchange 1 | Keyboard and screen-reader access; plain-language mathematics; explicit self and partner outcomes |
| Money Exchange 2 | Keyboard and screen-reader access; explicit give/take direction; labeled fairness scale |
| Faces | Keyboard access; scalable interface; neutral image identifiers; extended-time form; no claim of nonvisual equivalence |

For the mock:

- Select accessibility settings before starting.
- Record changes between games.
- Treat unsupported mid-game changes as invalidating that game's comparison.
- Permit additional accommodation breaks.
- Do not rank standard and accommodated forms against each other.

## 13. Faces assets and validity

Faces is a required launch game. Its asset problem must be solved early and
time-boxed rather than allowed to become an indefinite blocker.

### Public Beta 0.1 requirements

- Legally usable original, licensed, or appropriately generated imagery.
- Documented provenance for every image.
- Appropriate releases and commercial web-use rights.
- No scraped, JobTestPrep, or Pymetrics images.
- Human review for obvious ambiguity.
- Human review for obvious representation problems.
- Removal or revision of clearly defective items.
- Clear disclosure that the activity supports format familiarization and does not
  provide a validated emotional-intelligence score.

### Representation review

Review obvious issues involving:

- Age.
- Skin tone.
- Gender presentation.
- Facial morphology.
- Relevant disability representation.
- Lighting and image quality.
- Background and clothing confounds.

### Label review

- Use more than one human reviewer.
- Record obvious disagreement and ambiguity.
- Remove poorly agreed items.
- Review cultural assumptions in context text.
- Avoid presenting intended labels as unquestionable psychological ground truth.

Full psychometric, demographic, or cross-cultural validation is not required for
Public Beta 0.1 because the product makes no such claim.

## 15. Scientific and ethical position

### Public Beta 0.1 reporting boundary

Reports may describe only what happened in Assessment Practice tasks, including:

- Accuracy.
- Errors and omissions.
- Response time and variability.
- Consistency within the task.
- Choices.
- Moves.
- Outcomes.
- Performance by task block or condition.
- Change between Assessment Practice forms.

Anything broader must be omitted or labeled as an unvalidated hypothesis.

### Public Beta 0.1 does not need to establish

- Psychometric validity.
- Employer-score equivalence.
- Workplace-performance prediction.
- Demographic fairness as a hiring instrument.
- Stable personality measurement.
- Near or far transfer.
- Training transfer to employment outcomes.
- Calibrated alternate-form equivalence.

These are not release blockers because the product does not make these claims.

### Prohibited claims

- Stable personality change.
- Employer fit.
- Hiring prediction.
- Proprietary-score replication.
- Employer pass thresholds.
- Guaranteed improvement or selection.

### Ethical rules

- Lead with observed task behavior.
- Show strengths and trade-offs together where used.
- Never prescribe a supposedly desirable employer personality.
- Do not use anxiety-based sales tactics.
- Keep familiarization, training, and psychological interpretation distinct.

## 17. Proportionate release gates

### Blocking for Public Beta 0.1

#### Functional completeness

- All twelve games are playable individually.
- The full mock completes and resumes correctly.
- Guides, examples, reports, replay, and history work.
- Stop, Balloons, and Towers include Advanced Training Mode.
- Invalid and interrupted sessions are labeled correctly.

#### Descriptive correctness

- Displayed results can be reproduced from recorded events.
- Reports describe Assessment Practice task behavior only.
- No unsupported inference is presented as fact.

#### Rights and originality

- Code and assets are original or legally usable.
- Asset provenance is documented.
- Faces imagery is rights-cleared.
- Protected competitor expression is not copied.

#### Core accessibility

- Critical navigation and controls support keyboard operation where the task
  permits it.
- Color is not the only signal.
- Focus and labels are visible and readable.
- Reduced motion is available where appropriate.
- Known non-equivalent accommodations are disclosed.
- Serious blockers in instructions, controls, and reports are resolved.

#### Privacy and integrity

- Local storage behavior is explained.
- Local data can be deleted.
- Optional analytics do not block use.
- Unnecessary sensitive candidate data is not collected.
- Resume and interruption behavior cannot create misleading results.

#### Claims and independence

- Beta status is visible.
- Employer and provider independence is explicit.
- No pass-threshold, employer-fit, personality, hiring, proprietary-score, or
  workplace-performance claim appears.
- Candidate invitations are described as authoritative.

#### Professional quality

- No known broken game state remains.
- No placeholder copy or unapproved asset remains.
- Supported responsive layouts work.
- Errors provide a recoverable path.
- Visual quality is credible for a professional candidate-preparation beta.

### Not blocking for Public Beta 0.1

- Psychometric or predictive validation.
- Workplace-outcome research.
- Demographic fairness validation as a hiring instrument.
- Stable-trait measurement.
- Transfer evidence.
- Calibrated alternate-form equivalence.
- Full cross-cultural validation.
- Employer-score replication.
- Completion of every planned employer page.

### Gates for stronger future claims

Evidence must be proportionate to the claim. For example:

- **Comparable alternate forms** requires form-calibration evidence.
- **Improvement beyond practice effects** requires an appropriate user study.
- **Broader capability relevance** requires construct evidence.
- **Workplace-performance relevance** requires criterion evidence.

Until then, reports remain descriptive.

## 20. Dependencies that affect timing

### Critical dependencies

#### Approved game specifications

Trial counts, timings, mechanics, reports, assets, and edge cases must be decided
before implementation. The master strategy cannot substitute for these packets.

#### Faces assets

Choose early among commissioned, licensed, or appropriately generated imagery.
Rights and production lead time can affect the public-beta date.

#### Shared-engine quality

Weak timing, state, event, or storage foundations will cause rework across twelve
games. The internal prototype must test them first.

#### Advanced Training specifications

Stop, Balloons, and Towers need approved pattern definitions, drill rules, drill
mechanics, retest rules, and comparison language.

#### Founder decision speed

A solo workflow depends on rapid resolution of mechanics, copy, visual direction,
edge cases, public naming, and scope disputes.

#### Browser and device behavior

Timing-sensitive games require real-browser testing for throttling, backgrounding,
mobile keyboards, and browser timer behavior.

### Targeted specialist dependencies

- Trademark and employer-name review.
- Faces licensing and commercial-use review.
- Accessibility review of critical journeys.
- Scientific review only for language extending beyond task description.
- Privacy review if server analytics are enabled.

### Non-blocking dependencies

- Employer-page expansion.
- Psychometric validation.
- Calibrated alternate forms.
- Workplace-outcome studies.
- Additional Advanced Training modules.
- Stronger capability claims.
- Accounts, payments, or cross-device storage.

## 21. Beta success metrics

All targets are operating hypotheses, not external benchmarks.

### Technical quality

- All twelve games satisfy launch acceptance criteria.
- At least 95% of valid individual-game starts complete without technical failure.
- Fewer than 3% of sessions experience unrecoverable state loss.
- At least 90% of mock resumes restore the correct next-game state.

### Practice adoption

- At least 70% of guide starters complete the selected game.
- At least 60% of users complete two or more games.
- At least 40% of full-mock starters complete all twelve games.
- At least 50% replay or open another game after a completion.

### Advanced Training value

Among eligible Stop, Balloons, and Towers users:

- At least 50% of report viewers start the recommended drill.
- At least 40% of drill starters finish it.
- At least 35% complete an unseen retest within seven days.
- At least 60% of retest completers say training added value beyond familiarization.
- At least 50% say the drill was clearly connected to their result.

### Understanding and trust

- At least 80% of tested users can explain one report limitation.
- Fewer than 10% believe the product predicts employer selection.
- Fewer than 10% infer employer endorsement.
- At least 80% understand why only three games have Advanced Training Mode.

The strategy succeeds only if users value both the complete Practice Library and
the deeper supported training.

## 23. Final implementation brief

Build a lean, original, local-first candidate-preparation web application for
English-speaking undergraduate and recent-graduate candidates preparing for
early-career finance recruitment. Investment banking is the first specialized
pathway.

### Required Public Beta 0.1 scope

- Twelve complete Practice Mode games.
- Individual-game access.
- Guides, examples, and comprehension checks.
- One complete twelve-game mock.
- Basic descriptive reports for every game.
- Replay and local history.
- Advanced Training Mode for Stop, Balloons, and Towers.
- Responsive, professional interface.
- Accessible core interaction.
- Rights-cleared assets and original code.
- Clear beta, independence, and limitation language.

### Architecture mandate

Build shared infrastructure first:

- Application and game shell.
- Game and form registry.
- Instruction and example framework.
- Comprehension checks.
- Timing and trial engine.
- Input abstraction.
- Event recording.
- Descriptive scoring.
- Report framework.
- Local progress.
- Accessibility settings.
- Interruption handling.
- Mock orchestration.
- Form-exposure and version tracking.
- Advanced-training feature flags.

Use reusable engines for discrete timed response, repeated action, sequential
memory, repeated decisions, puzzle state, and contextual images.

### Specification mandate

This master strategy is not an exact game specification. Before implementing a
game, create and obtain founder approval for its mechanics, trial counts, timings,
controls, events, scoring, report, assets, accessibility, edge cases, and
acceptance tests.

The build agent must not invent unspecified product mechanics. It must surface
missing decisions before implementation.

### Required implementation order

1. Game Specification Packets and shared infrastructure.
2. All twelve Practice Mode games.
3. Full mock.
4. Basic reports and local history.
5. Advanced Training Mode for Stop, Balloons, and Towers.
6. Employer pages and expanded content.
7. Public-beta hardening.

### Reporting mandate

Public-beta reports may describe only behavior in Assessment Practice tasks:
accuracy, timing, consistency, choices, moves, outcomes, and differences between
our forms. Broader interpretations must be omitted or labeled as unvalidated
hypotheses.

### Employer mandate

Retain the employer-evidence framework, investment-banking pathway, planned
JPMorgan and Blackstone pages, invitation override, and independence language.
Employer content must not change scores or delay the working game product.

### Prohibited implementation and claims

- Proprietary Pymetrics scoring.
- Employer benchmarks or pass thresholds.
- Ideal personality profiles.
- Personality diagnosis.
- Hiring or workplace-performance prediction.
- Candidate rankings or unsupported percentiles.
- AI psychological interpretation.
- Guaranteed improvement.

### Public Beta 0.1 completion condition

Public Beta 0.1 is ready when all required product features work, descriptive
results are correct, serious usability and core accessibility blockers are
resolved, assets and code are original or rights-cleared, privacy behavior is
clear, claims remain within the evidence, and the product looks and behaves like
a professional candidate-preparation beta.

Psychometric validation and stronger evidence belong to the evidence-upgraded
version, not the Public Beta 0.1 launch gate.
