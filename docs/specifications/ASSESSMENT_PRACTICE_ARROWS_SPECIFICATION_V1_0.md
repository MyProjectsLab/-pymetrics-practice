## 1. Purpose, provenance and claims

Arrows is an original, rule-cued visual response exercise. Each display has five positions. A printed cue tells the candidate whether to follow the **one center arrow** or the **matching two outer arrows**, then the candidate responds Left or Right. Other positions either point the same way, point the opposite way, or contain neutral bars. The basic report describes responses, omissions and browser-observed times by cue and visual condition. It cannot identify a stable trait, diagnose attention, predict hiring, measure an employer's score, or prove that a change between runs is learning.

The private JobTestPrep inventory documents a 135-trial five-arrow task with a color-dependent center/side rule, congruent, incongruent and neutral trials, a fixation cross, and a correct-by-type report. This is a reference for familiar candidate flow, not a verified contract for current employer assessments. Assessment Practice uses text-and-shape rule cues, original layouts/copy, 120 trials, original order manifests and descriptive-only scoring. No JobTestPrep or Pymetrics text, art, code, normative score, or claimed trait inference is reused.

## 2. Scored form and display

Six fixed, versioned forms `AR1`–`AR6` are supplied in `ASSESSMENT_PRACTICE_ARROWS_FORMS_V1_0.json`; `scripts/generate-arrows-forms.mjs` is the reproducible constructor. Each form has **120 trials in five blocks of 24**. Every block contains four trials for each of the six `rule × condition` cells; within each cell two correct directions are Left and two Right. Thus each completed form contains 60 center and 60 outer trials; 40 aligned, 40 opposed and 40 neutral trials; 20 of each rule-condition; and 60 Left and 60 Right answers. A form is an order variation, not a calibrated equivalent of another form.

The cue and symbols appear together at confirmed stimulus onset:

| Cue | Target positions | Distractors |
|---|---|---|
| `CENTER — follow the one middle arrow` with a marked central position | Position 3 | Positions 1, 2, 4 and 5 |
| `OUTER — follow the matching outer pair` with two marked outer positions | Positions 1 and 5; both always agree | Positions 2, 3 and 4 |

The target arrow(s) point in the manifest's `direction`. On **aligned** trials, every distractor arrow points that direction. On **opposed** trials, every distractor arrow points the other direction. On **neutral** trials, every distractor position is a vertical bar without a direction. Labels `CENTER`/`OUTER`, target-position frames and arrow/bar shapes carry the rule and condition; color may reinforce but never carries the only distinction. Scalable original SVG and text are used. The stimulus must not expose the correct response through hidden accessibility text during timed play.

The complete sequence has no run longer than five of the same rule or correct direction, including block boundaries. Each block has 8–15 rule switches among its 23 within-block transitions. For each rule-condition cell across a form, at least five trials follow a within-block rule switch and at least five follow a within-block rule stay. The first trial of every block is excluded from switch/stay analysis. All 15 form pairs differ at 101–115 of 120 playable `rule/condition/direction` positions; this is an order difference, not psychometric equating.

## 3. Form construction and assignment

The generator starts at unsigned seed `0xA2200001` and increments by one for `AR1` through `AR6`. Its xorshift32 step is exactly `state ^= state << 13; state ^= state >>> 17; state ^= state << 5`, applying unsigned 32-bit conversion after each complete step. For each candidate attempt, construct five identical 24-card multisets, each containing two copies of every rule/condition/direction tuple, and Fisher–Yates shuffle each block from the last index downward using `next() % (i + 1)`. Concatenate the five blocks, apply all §2 acceptance constraints and advance the same PRNG state across rejected candidates. The first accepted candidate is the form. Its stored `attempts` count and complete ordered trials must match the fixed JSON. Rejection is capped at 100,000 attempts per form and failure is fatal, never an invitation to silently substitute a form.

The SHA-256 of the canonical compact JSON object `{version,baseSeed,forms}` is `228b0e10405a80830c73e4b69e12950482967dec717f156c789e19fa11b1150b`. The JSON file also contains this digest. Verify the fixed file and regenerated bytes before use. No live generator can silently change a published form. Each trial stores its one-based global index and block number.

Standalone Practice assigns `AR1` through `AR6` in order by the durable **arm count** within the local installation, then cycles again. The atomic committed arm/counter transaction in §6 consumes exactly one rotation position even if Start cannot yet be confirmed or interruption happens before the first stimulus. A protected scored Start is accepted only after authoritative readback. An uncommitted or rejected arm does not consume a count. Individual deletion never decrements the counter; Delete all creates a replacement installation with counter zero. The candidate is told that sequences may repeat; no form is called unseen, new to the candidate or equivalent in difficulty. Deletion can remove local history, so the application makes no claim about earlier visits or other devices. Replay uses the next assigned form, not a promise of novelty. Mock assignment and interrupted-mock behavior follow §9.

## 4. Candidate journey and controls

Library → concise rule guide → two untimed worked examples (one CENTER, one OUTER, with aligned/opposed explanation) → two comprehension questions (which positions to follow under each cue, and when a response counts) → input setup/check → readiness → five scored blocks → report/history. Wrong answers show the rule and can be retried; they do not start a run. The examples, setup, readiness and between-block screens have no idle timeout. A returning shortcut may be added later only after a verified understanding record and fresh setup/input check; V1.0 does not require it.

Keyboard mode defaults to physical `ArrowLeft` and `ArrowRight` codes. The two bindings may be changed to distinct codes in this explicit allowlist: `ArrowLeft`, `ArrowRight`, `Space`, `KeyA`–`KeyZ`, `Digit0`–`Digit9`, `Numpad0`–`Numpad9`, `Comma`, `Period`, `Slash`, `Semicolon`, `Quote`, `BracketLeft`, `BracketRight`, `Backslash`, `Backquote`, `Minus`, `Equal`. Reject duplicate bindings, `event.repeat`, composition and any Ctrl/Alt/Meta/Shift chord. Tab, Enter, Escape, function/navigation/system keys and all modifier codes are unavailable as response bindings. Each direction needs a fresh, unmodified keydown/keyup browser check; display the captured physical code and human label. During timed play, fresh keydowns of the selected codes are canonical actions; native repeated/held keydowns are ignored, and keyup re-arms each key. The first eligible direction keydown in a trial is the scored response; later direction actions are retained as extras and cannot replace it. Simultaneous keys are ordered by handler-observed monotonic time and event sequence. Other raw key codes are neither used nor retained. Browser scrolling from the selected keys is prevented only during timed play; untimed buttons retain Space and Enter semantics. A selected key held from Start/Continue or during fixation cannot become a response on onset; it must be released and pressed again.

Pointer/touch mode presents large Left and Right controls and is a separately labeled input class. Admit only primary `pointerdown` on those controls with `button === 0` and a single active pointer ID; mouse, touch and pen are recorded as distinct sources. A second pointer while one is active is ignored and recorded as a multi-pointer quality event without scoring. The corresponding `click` is never a second response. Pointerup/cancel captured at window level ends that press even outside the original control; a press held across onset must be released before a fresh response. A pointerdown on Start/Continue is never a direction action. Pointer-only mode does not convert keyboard activation of a direction button into a timed response. An alternative or assistive input declaration is admissible only when its output is a supported unmodified keydown/keyup pair in keyboard mode or a supported primary pointerdown/up pair in pointer mode, each verified on both directions before Start. Click-only, synthesized-button-only or unknown-output methods cannot enter timed play in V1.0; explain the limitation and retain access to untimed preparation. The declared alternative profile is labeled as potentially delayed, never silently pooled with standard input. Nonvisual timed play is not represented as equivalent: before scored Start, state plainly that this visual timed task is not currently supported with screen-reader interpretation of the arrows; keep untimed guide/examples and other games available. A candidate who declares that they need spoken arrow interpretation cannot start the timed Arrows run in this beta.

During each trial, the cue and all five symbols are visible together. There is no score or correctness feedback during a scored block. A block summary gives correct/wrong/omitted counts and an explicit Continue action. The next block starts only after Continue and a fresh readiness check. Focus moves to each untimed heading and actionable control; timed stimulus announces a concise cue, not a rapid speech stream of five symbols. Reduced motion removes nonessential transitions. Zoom/reflow must preserve the entire cue, symbol row and controls without horizontal scrolling at 200% on the supported desktop viewport. Breaks between blocks are untimed and may be paused. The complete input profile—input class, pointer source or both physical key codes, alternative-input declaration, shape/display accommodation and reduced-motion selection—is frozen at the accepted arm for all five blocks. In-app profile controls stay locked until the attempt ends. A detected profile change at a break makes the attempt invalid and review-only; it cannot be repaired by a new input check or silently reported using the final binding. Browser zoom may change during a break only if the visibility/layout predicate in §5 still passes. The same profile is shown with every block and report; a new attempt is required to use changed settings.

## 5. Timing, input and validity

Use `performance.now()` as the one monotonic clock for an attempt while its document is alive; record a new clock-domain ID after reload and never subtract timestamps across domains. All times below are sampled in the handler/animation-frame callback, not taken from browser event timestamps or backdated to scheduled times. A Start request first acquires exclusive live-run ownership, passes admission and durably arms as §6 defines; Start is **accepted only after** that transaction is authoritatively confirmed. Acceptance begins protection at `startMs`. Continue after an untimed break rechecks admission/ownership and begins the next protected block at its own `startMs`; it does not allocate a new form. Write the first fixation immediately, and confirm its connected visible display on the next animation frame at `fixationMs`. This is trial 1's **800-ms** fixation in **every** block. The first stimulus is due at `fixationMs + 800`; each later fixation is written immediately after the previous confirmed offset, confirmed at `fixationMs`, and its stimulus is due at `fixationMs + 700`. A delayed but still valid fixation confirmation shifts its own stimulus due time; it is never backdated. Confirm the cue and five symbols on a frame at `onsetMs ≥ stimulusDueMs`. Accept the first fresh selected-direction action at `onsetMs ≤ actionMs < onsetMs + 1,500`; an action at the exact deadline is not a response. Keep the stimulus visible throughout that window even after a response. The resolution due time is exactly `onsetMs + 1,500`. A callback before that due time waits. At its actual handler time `resolutionMs ≥ resolutionDueMs`, resolve omission if no eligible response, request removal of cue/symbols immediately, and confirm their absence in a frame at `offsetMs ≥ resolutionMs`. A selected action at or after the resolution due time but before confirmed offset is recorded as a boundary extra, never scored or backdated. The fifth block ends after its last confirmed offset; no trailing fixation follows any block. The nominal complete scored display is about 4.4 minutes plus browser frames and untimed breaks.

For each fixation, stimulus, resolution and offset, record `dueMs`, actual confirmation/handler time and `latenessMs = max(0, actualMs − dueMs)`. Fixation due time is `startMs` for block-first trials and the prior `offsetMs` for later trials. Stimulus and resolution due times are defined above; offset due time is the **original resolution due time**, never the later removal request. A lateness **greater than 250 ms** for any of these stages, or a foreground frame-to-frame gap **greater than 250 ms** while protected, is a severe invalidation. Exactly 250 ms is not severe. A lateness or foreground gap **greater than 50 ms through 250 ms**, inclusive at 250, records a non-severe timing note; affected trial timing is excluded from every median, IQR and contrast even if the response preceded the note. Fixation lateness affects its following trial. Offset lateness and a protected frame gap affect the current trial. Lateness of 50 ms or less does not itself exclude a trial. A response under 100 ms also excludes that trial's time without changing its accuracy. Invalid attempts have no aggregate timing under §7.

For a required fixation/stimulus/offset frame that never confirms, an independently scheduled 2,000-ms watchdog is a fallback: measure from that stage's original due time and invalidate no later than the first runnable watchdog check at or after `dueMs + 2,000`. A callback that finally confirms later must not clear the invalidation. Any observed severe lateness invalidates immediately, before the fallback deadline. The watchdog is not a wall-clock promise during OS/browser suspension. Record a confirmed presentation and its measured onset before any immediate timing invalidation, even if the frame is late. A canceled pre-onset callback cannot create exposure; a confirmed onset remains in the draft. Resolve and offset only a still-active trial; late callbacks cannot backdate, close twice or create another onset.

The protected period begins when a scored block Start/Continue is accepted and ends after that block's last confirmed offset or invalidation. Hiding, blur, freeze, page exit, viewport/layout failure, ownership loss, input-profile/binding change, or a timing failure during protection invalidates the attempt. A hide during an untimed example, readiness or between-block screen does not itself invalidate; recheck visibility/focus and the frozen profile before the next scored block. An invalid attempt is review-only and restarts from Block 1. A page reload never resumes a timed block or a between-block break; the existing attempt becomes incomplete/review-only and a new accepted arm begins at Block 1. A canceled or late browser-owner acquisition cannot strand the lock; release ownership on terminal state or canceled entry, including when a storage callback never delivers. Input handling and trial resolution never await storage. After an offset, a late callback cannot create another response or onset.

Viewport admission is based on visible layout rather than a fixed pixel claim. Before Start/Continue, measure the reserved stimulus footprint containing the future cue, all five symbols and both direction controls (where pointer mode is selected). During fixation, measure that footprint plus the visible fixation; during presentation, measure the actual cue/symbols/controls. At each admission/resize/orientation/visual-viewport check and each protected frame, the applicable elements must be mounted, non-hidden, non-occluded by an app overlay, horizontally within the layout viewport with no page horizontal overflow, and vertically inside the visual viewport without a timed-play scroll. The same predicate applies at 200% zoom on the supported desktop viewport; zoom is permitted only when it passes. Failed admission before protection disables Start/Continue with an actionable resize/zoom explanation, and recovery allows a fresh check. Failure during protection invalidates, preserving the draft. A 320-pixel responsive preview is a development check, not a blanket mobile-device qualification.

## 6. Events, storage and recovery

Version specification `arrows_spec_v1_0`, fixed form inventory `arrows_forms_v1`, presentation plan `arrows_timing_v1`, input profile `arrows_input_v1`, event schema `arrows_event_v1`, storage `arrows_storage_v1`, and scoring `arrows_scoring_v1`. The per-attempt ordered timed journal records at least: `prepared`, `form_assigned`, `armed`, `fixation_confirmed`, `stimulus_presented`, first response or omission, ignored/repeated/extra selected-direction actions, key/pointer release, `stimulus_offset`, `trial_closed`, block summary/continuation, lifecycle/quality events, `invalidated` or `session_completed`. An `armed` or `block_continued` event records the complete frozen profile and clock-domain ID. Each fixation/presentation/resolution/offset event records the stage's due time, actual time, timing note/severity and trial identity; each response records direction, input class/source, physical binding or pointer type, and handler-observed time. Every timed event has session/form identity, contiguous sequence number and monotonic timestamp within one clock domain. A separate versioned activity continuation records `report_viewed` with wall-clock time, unique view ID, installation/session identity and source surface; it never changes timed event bytes or the scored result. Do not record unrelated raw keys, account/employer identity, or proprietary scoring.

The Start request performs one IndexedDB read-write transaction over the installation root: validate its version and installation ID, allocate `AR((scoredStartCount mod 6)+1)`, increment that count once, and insert a unique armed attempt containing installation/session/form IDs, all recorded versions, the frozen setup/profile, a new clock-domain ID, start request identity, and the initial ordered `prepared`/`form_assigned`/`armed` journal prefix. Transaction completion **plus authoritative readback** of that exact attempt/counter is required before accepting Start or writing fixation. A rejected/pre-commit transaction leaves the count unchanged and no run starts. If the transaction commits but its callback does not deliver, readback can recover the armed attempt; until it succeeds, show “Start could not be confirmed” and do not start timing or silently allocate another form. A canceled request cannot later start a run. Once a committed arm is verified, its form count remains consumed even if the user cancels before fixation. Use one start request ID to deduplicate retries. An acquisition or readback that never resolves must be cancelable without holding the browser-owner lock indefinitely; fence late callbacks and abort still-active transactions where possible. If storage is unavailable, timed Start is unavailable in V1.0; no unlabeled temporary run is offered.

Keep live events synchronously in memory with immutable sequence order; input resolution and display callbacks never await storage. At each between-block summary, after protection ends, atomically checkpoint the complete known journal prefix, closed-trial count and profile under the same attempt/version/revision. Continue is enabled only after that checkpoint is authoritatively confirmed; if it fails or remains uncertain, the candidate can retry while untimed or end with a review-only draft. At invalidation, terminal exit or successful completion, copy the current immutable snapshot first and then attempt an out-of-timing write. A failed invalidation/exit write leaves a clearly labeled in-tab draft and a retry; the last committed prefix remains the recoverable floor. A mid-block crash/reload may lose events since the last committed checkpoint, including an actual displayed stimulus; the armed/checkpoint record is labeled **incomplete with later display status unknown**, never “zero exposures” or a completed score. A confirmed onset is retained immediately in memory even if invalidation occurs in the same callback. After reload, an armed or incomplete attempt is review-only; **neither a timed block nor a between-block break resumes**, and a new accepted arm restarts at Block 1. Where a complete result's write committed but delivery was lost, authoritative readback verifies exact immutable bytes before claiming saved; if readback is unavailable, show uncertain/unsaved status and withhold durable claims. A completion-write failure leaves a reviewable in-tab result with truthful unsaved copy and one retry. Validate exact manifest/version, event order, display/response feasibility, timing/quality flags, frozen profile, immutable summary and deletion/installation authority before any saved claim. Migrate versioned storage explicitly and non-destructively; unsupported historical records remain review-only rather than silently rescored.

Individual deletion atomically removes the selected attempt/result/activity and records a session tombstone in the current installation; it **does not** reset the scored-start counter. Delete all atomically replaces the Arrows installation ID, resets the counter to zero, and removes all Arrows attempts/results/activity/tombstones. Preserve other games' records. Each delayed start, draft, completion, retry or report-view write must recheck installation ID, session tombstone and current revision within its authoritative transaction; an older callback cannot restore deleted bytes or write into the replacement installation. After deletion, the active tab withdraws the deleted report before showing numbers again; absent/delayed cross-tab notifications cannot confer saved status because opening or resuming a report requires a fresh authoritative read. Cross-tab notifications only improve promptness. The small practice inventory is not scarce, so neither individual deletion nor Delete all justifies a fresh/unseen label. History distinguishes valid, invalid, incomplete, modified-key, alternative-input and repeated-form attempts. No server account or optional analytics is needed for the game.

Each genuine opening of a verified report creates a new random view ID; retrying that same opening uses the same ID and is idempotent per `(installationId, sessionId, viewId)`. An atomic activity append checks that the result still exists and has the exact expected immutable identity, then stores one `report_viewed` event with the next activity sequence. Multiple independent openings may each append one event. A failed/undelivered append is never fabricated in history; retry/readback uses the same view ID. Individual/Delete-all retirement also fences this append. Activity timestamps are wall-clock observations, never mixed into timed response-time arithmetic.

## 7. Reconciliation and basic report

A complete form must reconcile exactly 120 `stimulus_presented` and 120 `trial_closed` identities, one first response or omission per trial, five block boundaries, exact manifest tuples, and `correct + wrong + omitted = 120`. A wrong response is the first eligible direction opposite the target; omission means no eligible direction before the deadline. Early/boundary/extra inputs never alter that classification. Every attempt, including a partial one, reconciles `presented = closed + unresolvedPresented` and `120 = presented + unpresented`; `closed = correct + wrong + omitted` only for genuinely closed trials. An observed first response on an unresolved presented trial remains an event, **not** a scored correct/wrong classification. Never classify an unpresented or unresolved trial as an omission. An explicit invalidation makes status Invalid even if 120 trials later appear closed; a crash/reload without a terminal event makes status Incomplete. Only a complete, reconciled, non-invalidated attempt may be Valid. Store raw times; display milliseconds rounded half away from zero to integers, with negative zero shown as zero. Display count/denominator percentages half-up to one decimal place from unrounded integer fractions. No aggregate result is labeled valid if the journal contains invalidation or an unresolved quality gate.

For **Valid** reports only, show overall correct/120, wrong/120 and omitted/120; the same counts by CENTER/OUTER (60 each), aligned/opposed/neutral (40 each) and each rule-condition (20 each). Show each denominator beside its count. For Invalid/Incomplete review, suppress **all** aggregate accuracy percentages, per-cell classification tables, medians, IQRs and contrasts, even if 120 closures were recorded. Instead show status/reason and factual `presented`, `closed`, `unresolvedPresented`, `unpresented` counts, plus an ordered event review with observed responses and a warning that live events after the last durable checkpoint may be missing. A zero denominator displays “No closed trials,” never `0%`. Clean correct-response times in a Valid attempt are `firstEligibleActionMs − confirmedOnsetMs` for correct trials with **100 ≤ RT < 1,500 ms**, no >50-ms stage/frame timing note affecting the trial under §5, and the one frozen recorded input profile. A response under 100 ms remains scored for accuracy but is excluded from timing summaries and identified as too early for timing interpretation. Show a median from at least five clean times in that reported cell. For five through seven, show the median and “Not enough clean responses for variability.” Show median-of-halves IQR from at least eight. For fewer than five, show “Not enough clean responses for a time summary.” Zero timings never produce a fabricated median.

For switch/stay, compare only trials 2–24 of each block to the preceding trial's rule; omit the first trial of every block (five trials total). Show accuracy/counts for switch and stay with their actual denominators. A descriptive switch-time difference requires at least ten clean correct responses in each cohort and is `median(raw switch RTs) − median(raw stay RTs)`. A descriptive opposed-minus-aligned time difference likewise requires at least ten clean correct responses in each cohort and is `median(raw opposed RTs) − median(raw aligned RTs)`. Sort unrounded times ascending. For even `n`, the median is the arithmetic mean of the two central values. For odd `n`, the median is the central value and **is excluded from both quartile halves**; Q1/Q3 are the medians of the lower/upper halves, and IQR is `Q3 − Q1`. Calculate both contrasts from unrounded medians and round the final signed difference once; never subtract already rounded display values. These differences are **within this Assessment Practice sequence** and may reflect visual mix, familiarity or ordinary variation; do not label them as a latent ability or causal effect. Do not combine unlike input classes, key bindings, accommodation states, specification/scoring versions or uncalibrated form identities into an improvement claim. V1.0's frozen profile and form versions prevent within-run pooling, and separate run reports are descriptive, not an improvement classifier.

Every report includes valid/invalid/incomplete and saved/unsaved/uncertain status; form and scoring versions; the frozen response binding or pointer source and declared device/input/accessibility context; interruption reason or “none recorded”; the counts and supported time summaries **only when Valid**; a short task-limited practical observation where supported; a Replay action; explicit uncalibrated-form/physical-latency limitations; and “Advanced Training is not available for Arrows in this beta.” It must not show an employer score, pass/fail threshold, personality trait, percentile or guaranteed improvement. Opening a verified report appends one factual, idempotent `report_viewed` activity event under §6 without changing its timed result.

## 9. Full mock and scope boundary
