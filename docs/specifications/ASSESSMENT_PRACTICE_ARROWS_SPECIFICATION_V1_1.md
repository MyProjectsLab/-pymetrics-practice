## 3. Display and answer rule

Each trial presents one centered row of five equally sized original arrows or marks. Positions 1, 2, 4 and 5 are the **four sides**. One rule cue is visible with the row:

| Manifest color and condition | Visible row | Correct direction |
|---|---|---|
| `red`, `aligned` | Five red arrows pointing in the same direction | Shared direction of the four side arrows |
| `red`, `opposed` | Four red side arrows agree; red middle arrow points opposite | Shared direction of the four side arrows |
| `blue`, `aligned` | Five blue arrows pointing in the same direction | Direction of the middle arrow |
| `blue`, `opposed` | Four blue side arrows agree; blue middle arrow points opposite | Direction of the middle arrow |
| `black`, `neutral` | Black middle arrow; four horizontal, nondirectional black side marks | Direction of the middle arrow |

`direction` in the manifest is always the correct answer. For opposed blue trials the four side arrows point opposite `direction`; for opposed red trials only the middle arrow points opposite. For neutral trials, the four side marks are not arrows. No other color-condition pair is legal. The candidate sees no answer direction or correctness in hidden accessibility text while timing is protected.

The standard display uses color **and** a small original rule label, `SIDES` or `MIDDLE`, above the arrow row; no target-position frames appear during scored play. The label supplies the Master Strategy's required non-color cue. A separately labeled high-distinction accommodation may enlarge that label and add a pattern; its profile and result are marked modified and are not pooled with standard timing. Nonvisual interpretation of rapidly changing arrows is unavailable for timed play in this beta; keep the untimed guide accessible and explain this before Start. Use scalable vectors, strong contrast, reduced-motion support, remappable keys and the existing pointer alternative without implying identical timing across input classes. The frozen input/display profile and its eligibility checks follow V1.0 §4–5.

## 4. Fixed forms and construction

Six fixed scored forms `AR11-1` through `AR11-6` live in `ASSESSMENT_PRACTICE_ARROWS_FORMS_V1_1.json`; `scripts/generate-arrows-forms-v11.mjs` is their sole constructor. Each has **135 trials in five blocks of 27**. There are exactly **four** untimed between-block screens, after confirmed offsets of trials 27, 54, 81 and 108. This is an Assessment Practice pacing choice because the reference does not prove a break policy. There is no immediate correctness feedback during a scored block. Each of the first four blocks ends with a factual correct/wrong/omitted summary and an explicit Continue; the next block uses the V1.0 block-first fixation and fresh protected admission. Trial 135's confirmed offset transitions directly to terminal completion and its report, with no fifth break, Continue or Block 6. While terminal save is pending or unconfirmed, the report remains reviewable with truthful status and a retry as §5 requires.

Each form uses exactly 31 red aligned, 31 blue aligned, 27 red opposed, 27 blue opposed and 19 black neutral cards. Thus the observed-category denominators are **62 aligned, 54 opposed, 19 neutral**; colors are 58 red, 58 blue and 19 black; rules are 58 sides and 77 middle. These counts intentionally match one recorded practice result, not a claim that all provider forms share them. For each cell in the listed order, create `count` cards, assigning `direction = left` when `copy % 2 === cellIndex % 2`, otherwise `right`, with `copy` zero-based. This yields 68 Left and 67 Right answers. A form is a shuffled order of that deck, never an independently sampled new mix.

The constructor starts at unsigned seed `0xA2210001` and increments by one for forms 1–6. It uses the V1.0 xorshift32 step with unsigned conversion and Fisher–Yates shuffles the entire 135-card deck, drawing `next() % (i+1)` from last index down to 1. Rejected candidates consume that PRNG state; the first accepted candidate within 100,000 attempts is stored. Rejection is fatal after that limit. A candidate passes only if: (a) no run of the same answer or sides/middle rule exceeds five and no run of the same condition exceeds six, including block boundaries; (b) each 27-card block contains 9–16 aligned, 7–14 opposed, 1–7 neutral, 8–15 sides, 9–18 Left answers and 8–20 within-block rule switches; (c) each color-condition cell has at least three within-block rule-switch observations and three stays, excluding the first card of each block. Stored `rule`, one-based `block` and one-based `index` are derived after acceptance. All 15 form pairs differ at least 100 of 135 playable `color/condition/direction` positions. These are order variants, **not calibrated equivalent forms**.

The SHA-256 of compact `JSON.stringify({version,baseSeed,forms})` is `bedfa8c58fd9bde20cf7b9408b6ac5909c6bae45d025bff9c3de8041f8ae6855`; the JSON also stores that digest. Implementation must verify the fixed file, canonical digest and exact regenerated bytes. A production run loads the fixed file and never silently regenerates or substitutes a sequence.

## 5. Candidate journey, events and recovery

Library → short color/position guide → worked red-opposed, blue-opposed and black-neutral examples → comprehension check of red versus blue/black → response-key setup and browser input check → readiness → five scored blocks → simple report/history. The guide, examples, questions, setup, readiness and breaks are untimed. Wrong comprehension answers explain the rule and can be retried. The scored row keeps the video-like fixation/row rhythm; V1.0's exact timing and validity boundaries remain the product's original documented choices because the saved reference does not establish their values. A normal response uses the first fresh selected Left/Right action within the window; mistakes do not terminate a run. The game ends after trial 135 unless invalidated or voluntarily exited. Default physical codes are `ArrowLeft` and `ArrowRight`; remapping and pointer use follow V1.0. This product does not claim the reference consistently uses those keys.

The ordered journal stores exact form/spec/presentation/event/scoring identities and the manifest color, condition, correct direction, rule and five rendered symbols for each confirmed stimulus, plus the V1.0 timing, first-action, omission, extra-input, quality and lifecycle evidence. Rendered-symbol data are for internal reconciliation; the candidate report and history never expose unseen future form rows. The live engine and durable adapter must reject a color/rule/symbol/answer contradiction, missing or duplicated trial, replayed action identity, impossible order or malformed version. First-display evidence is recorded before any immediate invalidation. V1.0 arm/readback, checkpoint, terminal write, timeout/readback, retry, deletion, installation replacement and report-view authority all apply using V1.1 IDs. An uncertain write never creates a saved claim; a confirmed deletion withdraws visible numbers and retry surfaces before a later stale callback can restore them.

The 27-trial breaks are the only in-game checkpoint boundaries. Continue waits for an authoritative committed prefix and current installation/session authority. A protected block never waits for storage. Crash or reload within a block can lose the uncheckpointed tail and is labeled incomplete with later display unknown; neither a block nor a break resumes after reload. A new arm starts at trial 1 on its next assigned V1.1 form. A live invalid/complete result is reviewable while a save is uncertain and is called saved only after exact durable readback. History reopens the immutable versioned report and adds idempotent `report_viewed` activity only while the same result still exists. Cross-tab ownership and both deletion orders must match the durable root and visible UI.

## 6. Reconciliation and report

A valid complete V1.1 report requires exactly 135 distinct confirmed stimulus identities, 135 closed trials, five completed blocks, the fixed manifest sequence and no invalidation. `correct + wrong + omitted = 135`; `presented = closed + unresolvedPresented`; `135 = presented + unpresented`. A wrong action is the first eligible direction opposite the manifest answer; an omission has no eligible action before deadline. Do not call a late first action a correction or a missing/unresolved stimulus an omission. Invalid and incomplete attempts suppress aggregate percentages, medians, IQRs and contrasts as in V1.0.

The default report shows **overall correct/135** and correct/actual-denominator for aligned (62), opposed (54) and neutral (19), with wrong and omitted counts. In the same main results panel it also shows the **overall clean correct-response median**, its actual `n`, and overall clean IQR when supported. A small optional detail view may show red/blue/black, aligned/opposed/neutral and switch/stay counts **and clean correct-response medians/IQRs** with their actual denominators and timing `n`. The optional detail may also show switch-minus-stay and opposed-minus-aligned raw-median contrasts. Counts for all five legal cells and first eligible response times remain in the internal reconciled result. The V1.0 clean-time rule (`100 ≤ RT < 1,500 ms` and no timing note) and sample thresholds apply per displayed group: median from five, median-of-halves IQR from eight, differences from ten clean responses in each cohort. `switch` means sides versus middle changed from the prior trial **within the same block**; blue↔black is not a rule switch. All percentages are half-up to one decimal; times and contrasts use V1.0's raw-value rounding. At `n<5`, show “Not enough clean responses for a time summary” beside `n`; at `5≤n<8`, show the median and “Not enough clean responses for variability” beside `n`; at `n≥8`, show median and IQR beside `n`. Unsupported contrasts say “Not enough clean responses.” Never display a fabricated zero. Invalid/Incomplete review suppresses scored accuracy breakdowns, timing and contrasts while retaining factual presented/closed counts as above. Report copy says this was one Assessment Practice sequence, not an employer result; the observed 62/54/19 mix is not a provider norm. Old V1.0 and new V1.1 results remain visibly separate.
